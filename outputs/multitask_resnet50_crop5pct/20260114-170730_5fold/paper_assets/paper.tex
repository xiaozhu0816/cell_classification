\begin{abstract}
Automated analysis of microscopy time courses can support infection phenotyping by predicting both infection state and disease progression stage. We propose a multi-task deep learning approach that jointly predicts infection status and post-infection time from microscopy images. To further assess reliability over time, we compute performance within sliding temporal windows.

Because the data provider (GMU) noted an approximate \textasciitilde5\% field-of-view overlap between adjacent acquisition positions, we adopt a simple, robust leakage-mitigation preprocessing step in the final experiment: we remove the outer 5\% border of each frame (center crop; retaining the central 90\%\,$\times$\,90\% region) prior to resizing.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Task definition}
We train a multi-task model to predict (1) infection state (infected vs. mock/PBS) and (2) progression time (hours) from microscopy frames.

\subsection{Leakage mitigation via border cropping (final setting)}
To reduce potential near-duplicate content leakage caused by the reported \textasciitilde5\% field-of-view overlap, we center-crop each frame to remove the outer 5\% border before resizing to the model input size. This preprocessing is applied consistently to train/validation/test.
